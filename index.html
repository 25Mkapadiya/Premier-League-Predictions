Model/Methodology

How the forecast works

The site runs entirely on public no-key data and falls back cleanly whenever a public source has not updated yet.

01

Structural score model

Venue-adjusted attack and defense, Elo, Poisson scoring and Dixon-Coles low-score correction create the underlying score distribution.

02

Dynamic recent performance

Up to ten completed matches are compared with the model's prior expectations. Recent games count more, opponent strength matters, venue form is tracked separately, and samples are shrunk toward neutral.

03

xG & shot quality

Football-Data supplies results and shot statistics while public Understat league pages add xG/xGA when available. Shots on target are used as a capped fallback signal, never as a substitute for team strength.

04

Rest & fixture congestion

Rest-day differences and recent match load apply deliberately small, capped adjustments instead of overwhelming team quality.

05

Public market benchmark

When Football-Data publishes upcoming EPL odds, the bookmaker margin is removed first. The trained ensemble uses that market evidence only in the regime where it was historically validated.

06

Walk-forward learned ensemble

A time-ordered training workflow learns how much to trust structural, form, shots, rest and public market features, calibrates on an earlier slice, and reports a later holdout.

07

Coherent score markets

After the 1X2 ensemble is calibrated, the score lambdas are realigned so correct score, BTTS and totals remain consistent with the final match probabilities.

08

Forecast locking

At kickoff the prediction freezes. Results are graded against what the model knew beforehand, preventing hindsight leakage.

Evaluation policy

Accuracy has to survive unseen matches.

The currently enabled model is benchmarked on a time-ordered 2025/26 holdout. New features are only promoted after they improve out-of-sample probability quality.
