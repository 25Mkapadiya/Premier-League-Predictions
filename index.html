Model/Methodology

How the forecast works

The site uses the strongest data layer available for each match and falls back cleanly when an optional feed is missing.

01

Structural score model

Venue-adjusted attack and defense, Elo, Poisson scoring and Dixon-Coles low-score correction create the underlying score distribution.

02

Dynamic recent performance

Up to eight completed matches are compared with the model's prior expectations. Recent games count more, opponent strength matters, and samples are shrunk toward neutral.

03

xG, shots and availability

Historical match statistics are refreshed from Football-Data. Sportmonks xG and near-kickoff lineup/injury context are used when their secrets are configured; missing feeds are never guessed.

04

Rest & fixture congestion

Rest-day differences and recent match load apply deliberately small, capped adjustments instead of overwhelming team quality.

05

Fair bookmaker consensus

When current odds are available, bookmaker margins are removed first. The consensus then acts as a separate signal rather than being mistaken for raw probability.

06

Walk-forward learned ensemble

A time-ordered training workflow learns how much to trust structural, form, market and schedule features, calibrates probabilities on an earlier slice, and reports an untouched later holdout.

07

Coherent score markets

After the 1X2 ensemble is calibrated, the score lambdas are realigned so correct score, BTTS and totals remain consistent with the final match probabilities.

08

Forecast locking

At kickoff the prediction freezes. Results are graded against what the model knew beforehand, preventing hindsight leakage.

Evaluation policy

Accuracy has to survive unseen matches.

Baseline metrics remain visible until the trained v3 holdout exists. A new feature is only promoted when it improves probability quality outside its fitting window.
